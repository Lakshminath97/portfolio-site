03 — HOW I THINK

SOP → VR DESIGN · LOTO

How a procedure becomes a VR module

Every SOP step gets four things: a trigger, a validation check, the mistake it catches, and the feedback the trainee gets. Pick a step to see its design.

Example: simplified lockout/tagout (LOTO) of a process valve. Real procedures add steps such as notifying affected personnel, shutting down equipment and double block-and-bleed. Not taken from any client procedure.

STEP 01 · VR DESIGN

Identify the valve against the permit

Trigger
Trainee points at a valve and selects its tag plate.
Validation
The selected tag number must match the tag on the work permit held in the trainee's off-hand.
Mistake caught
Picking the identical-looking valve next to the right one.
Feedback
Wrong tag: plate outlines red, the permit line flashes and the controller pulses once. Right tag: plate locks in amber and the next step unlocks.

STEP 02 · VR DESIGN

Close the isolation valve

Trigger
Trainee grabs the handwheel with both hands and turns it.
Validation
Rotation direction is clockwise and the wheel reaches its fully-seated end stop.
Mistake caught
Turning the wrong way, or stopping part-way and assuming it is closed.
Feedback
Resistance builds through haptics as the valve seats; the position indicator moves to CLOSED with a solid click. Under-rotated: indicator stays between marks.

STEP 03 · VR DESIGN

Apply lock and tag

Trigger
Trainee takes a personal lock from the belt, places it on the valve hasp, then hangs a signed tag.
Validation
Lock snaps into the hasp (closed state) and a tag is attached to that same lock.
Mistake caught
Hanging a tag without a lock, or locking the wrong point.
Feedback
The procedure will not advance without the lock; a short supervisor voice line explains why a tag alone is not isolation.

STEP 04 · VR DESIGN

Release stored energy (bleed)

Trigger
Trainee opens the bleed valve downstream of the isolation point.
Validation
The local pressure gauge must read zero before the bleed valve is left and the next step begins.
Mistake caught
Moving on while the line is still under pressure.
Feedback
The gauge needle falls in real time with an audible hiss. Leaving early triggers a safe, simulated release and a pause-and-explain moment.

STEP 05 · VR DESIGN

Verify zero energy

Trigger
Trainee performs a try-test: attempts to start flow and re-checks the gauge.
Validation
No flow, gauge at zero, and the verification is confirmed on the checklist.
Mistake caught
Assuming isolation worked without proving it.
Feedback
Checklist line ticks in amber; the debrief shows each step, time taken and every mistake caught along the way.
